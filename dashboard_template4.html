The financing chain — and what is not yet measured

Why the return side is so thinly measured

Nearly every free, frequent series on this subject measures the bet. Capital spending, preleasing, connection queues, chip sales and bookings are all commitment — and chip sales in particular are the buildout money counted a second time one step up the chain, so they cannot be read as demand.

The return side is barely measured at all. The largest disclosed, auditable enterprise AI franchises in company filings come to under ${AI_ARR}bn between them, against the capital spending shown above. Reported figures from the model companies are much larger but unaudited, unfiled, and partly funded by the same capital cycle. A card-transaction panel covering US businesses reported the effective price paid per million tokens down {TOKEN_FALL}% since March (typed in {D_TOKEN}), which is the mechanism by which capacity can be fully used and still not pay for itself.

How much of each risk you can actually see

Three routes to contagion, three very different levels of disclosure. This decides how much weight each panel can carry.

Government borrowing — fully visible

Gilt yields, Bank Rate and SONIA are published daily by the Bank of England. Auction cover and issuance plans come from the DMO. Nothing material is hidden, and the panel reads it directly.

Gulf and energy — mostly visible

Prices are live and the physical flows can be counted: chokepoint transits are published daily by the IMF and the IEA. What stays hidden is inventory, especially strategic reserves — which is why the futures curve is the better read than any supply-and-demand estimate.

The 5.25% line, and why the split matters more

Above roughly 5.25%, shares and government bonds have historically stopped cushioning each other and started falling together. That is an observed pattern, not a cause. The usual explanation is that bonds stop hedging when inflation becomes the dominant shock, so the yield level is a symptom rather than a trigger.

That makes the split worth watching as well as the total. A rise in yields can come from the real yield and term premium (the cost of money) or from expected inflation. Both halves are in the tables above, for the US under Financial stress and for the UK under UK rates, with their three-month changes.

UK implied inflation is measured against RPI, which has historically run about a percentage point above CPI, so its level is not comparable with the American figure. The change over time is.

AI financing — largely invisible

Providers must disclose contracted backlog and any customer above a tenth of revenue, so their side is reasonably lit. The buyers are private companies with no disclosure duty, the contract terms are confidential, and the lending sits in private credit funds and joint ventures that publish nothing. You can see the size of the commitment but not the ability to meet it. Here the share prices do the work no filing does.

What the proxies actually are

Tanker charter rates are stood in for by two tanker owners' share prices. Credit spreads are stood in for by a high-yield bond fund against an investment-grade one. AI contract risk is stood in for by the share prices of the two most exposed names. UK gilts, Bank Rate, UK power and both labour series are the real thing.

What this cannot do

Nothing here tells you when to sell. Two measures moved ahead of past falls, by single-digit percentages, across three episodes. Read it as a description of conditions — and note that it currently argues in both directions.
